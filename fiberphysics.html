1. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

Total Internal Reflection occurs when a light ray traveling in a denser medium ($n_1$) strikes the boundary of a rarer medium ($n_2$) at an incident angle $\theta_1$ greater than the critical angle $\theta_c$.

θc = arcsin(n₂ / n₁)  |  Requires n₁ > n₂

2. Beer-Lambert Attenuation

Optical signal power decreases exponentially along the fibre length due to Rayleigh scattering and intrinsic absorption loss ($\alpha$).

P(L) = P₀ · 10^(-α · L / 10)

3. Numerical Aperture & Acceptance Cone

The Numerical Aperture (NA) measures the light-gathering capability of the fiber. Light launched within the acceptance cone angle $\theta_a$ will undergo TIR.

NA = √(n₁² - n₂²) = n₀ · sin(θa)

4. Macrobend Radiation Loss

When a fiber is bent beyond its critical curvature radius, light rays hit the core-cladding boundary at angles smaller than $\theta_c$, causing energy to leak into the cladding.

α_bend ∝ exp(-R / R_critical)

Optical Fiber Applications in Engineering & Industry

Comprehensive illustrative overview of practical optical guide systems

TELECOMMUNICATIONS Submarine & Terrestrial

Transoceanic Fibre Cable Systems

Single-mode optical fibers carry petabits of global internet traffic across ocean beds. Operating at the 1550 nm low-attenuation window, Erbium-Doped Fiber Amplifiers (EDFAs) boost signals every 50-80 km.

Key Formula: Attenuation α ≈ 0.18 dB/km @ 1550 nm
MEDICAL PHYSICS Minimally Invasive

Medical Endoscopes & Laser Surgery

Coherent fiber bundles transfer high-resolution images from inside human body cavities, while non-coherent fiber bundles deliver high-power illuminating laser light for precision surgery and diagnostic imaging.

Key Parameter: High NA (≥0.5) for max light acceptance
SENSING & STRUCTURAL FBG Sensors

Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) Strain Sensors

FBGs are microscopic periodic variations in core refractive index. Applied strain or temperature shifts the reflected Bragg wavelength, providing real-time health monitoring for bridges, aircraft wings, and dams.

Key Formula: λ_Bragg = 2 · n_eff · Λ
NAVIGATION & AEROSPACE Sagnac Effect

Fiber Optic Gyroscopes (FOG)

FOGs use the Sagnac interferometric effect with counter-propagating laser beams in a fiber coil to measure angular rotation rate with high precision without moving mechanical parts.

Key Formula: ΔΦ = (8π · A · N / λ · c) · Ω
